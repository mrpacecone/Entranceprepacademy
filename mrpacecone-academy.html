⚖ Introduction to Law and Kinds of Law

Covers the definition, nature, and characteristics of law. Introduces jurisprudence and the major classification of laws tested in BALLB entrance.

📖 Notes
🎯 MCQ Practice
✍ Short Answers

What is Law?

Law is a system of rules and regulations created and enforced by social or governmental institutions to regulate behavior. It has been defined differently by various schools of jurisprudence.

Key Definitions

  • Austin: "Law is a command issued by a sovereign, backed by a sanction."
  • Salmond: "Law is the body of principles recognized and applied by the state in the administration of justice."
  • Holland: "Law is a general rule of external human action enforced by a sovereign political authority."
  • Roscoe Pound: Law is a means of social engineering to satisfy the maximum of wants with minimum friction.

Nature and Characteristics of Law

  • Normative: Law prescribes how people ought to behave
  • General: Applies to all persons without discrimination
  • Territorial: Operates within a definite territory
  • Enforced by State: Backed by sovereign authority and sanctions
  • Dynamic: Law changes with society's needs

Types of Law

Civil Law vs Criminal Law: Civil law governs disputes between private parties; criminal law punishes acts that violate public order.

Constitutional Law: The supreme law that establishes the structure of government and guarantees fundamental rights.

Administrative Law: Governs the activities of government agencies.

Public Law vs Private Law: Public law regulates the relationship between individuals and the state; private law governs relationships between individuals.

Procedural Law vs Substantive Law: Substantive law defines rights and duties; procedural law prescribes the process for enforcing those rights.

Source: TU Faculty of Law Syllabus | Reference: Mero Adalat BALLB Syllabus Guide | Last Updated: 2025

📖 Constitution of Nepal

Complete coverage of constitutional development, organs of state, separation of powers, and checks and balances.

📖 Notes
📜 Key Articles
🎯 MCQ

Constitution of Nepal 2015 (2072 BS)

The Constitution of Nepal 2015 is the fundamental law of Nepal, promulgated on 20 September 2015 (3 Asoj 2072 BS). It replaced the Interim Constitution 2007.

Key Features

  • Federal Democratic Republic with 3 tiers of government
  • 7 Provinces, 77 Districts, 753 Local Bodies
  • 35 Fundamental Rights guaranteed
  • Independent judiciary with Supreme Court at apex
  • Bicameral federal parliament: House of Representatives + National Assembly
  • Constitutional Supremacy established

Constitutional Development of Nepal

  • 1948: Government of Nepal Act (first constitution)
  • 1951: Interim Government of Nepal Act
  • 1959: Constitution of Nepal (multiparty democracy)
  • 1962: Constitution of Nepal (Panchayat system)
  • 1990: Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal (parliamentary democracy)
  • 2007: Interim Constitution of Nepal
  • 2015: Constitution of Nepal (current)

Organs of State

Legislature (Parliament): Bicameral – House of Representatives (275 members) and National Assembly (59 members). Power to make laws.

Executive (Government): President as constitutional head; Prime Minister as executive head leading the Council of Ministers.

Judiciary: Supreme Court, High Courts (7), and District Courts (77). Ensures rule of law and protects fundamental rights.

Source: Constitution of Nepal 2015 | Reference: nlc.edu.np | Last Updated: 2025

👨‍⚖️ Jurists & Legal Theories

Jurist profiles and theories are repeatedly tested in BALLB entrance. Master all major schools of jurisprudence.

A

John Austin

Analytical / Positivist School

Command Theory: Law is a command of the sovereign, backed by sanction. Separates law from morality.

S

John Salmond

Analytical School

Law as body of principles recognized by the state in the administration of justice. Rights and duties central.

P

Roscoe Pound

Sociological School

"Law is Social Engineering." Law serves to balance competing individual and social interests.

K

Hans Kelsen

Pure Theory of Law

Law as a "normative science." Basic norm (Grundnorm) at apex of hierarchy. Law separated from morality.

H

H.L.A. Hart

Neo-Positivist School

Primary rules (obligations) + Secondary rules (rule of recognition, change, adjudication). Concept of Law (1961).

Sv

Friedrich Savigny

Historical School

Law grows with the nation's spirit (Volksgeist). Law is not made but found. Evolved from customs.

B

Jeremy Bentham

Utilitarian School

Greatest happiness of the greatest number. Law as instrument of utility. Father of modern jurisprudence.

Ho

Thomas Hobbes

Natural Law / Social Contract

Law is command of the sovereign. Social contract theory. State of nature: "Nasty, brutish and short."

M

Karl Marx

Marxist / Economic School

Law as superstructure of economic base. Law serves the ruling class. Withering away of law under communism.

AJohn Austin
BHans Kelsen
CRoscoe Pound
DSalmond

Explanation

Roscoe Pound of the Sociological school of jurisprudence said "Law is Social Engineering." He viewed law as a tool to balance competing social interests and maximize satisfaction with minimum friction. This is among the most frequently asked questions in BALLB entrance.

2. Nepal adopted its current Federal Democratic Republican system under which constitution?
AConstitution of Nepal 1990
BInterim Constitution 2007
CConstitution of Nepal 2015
DConstitution of Nepal 1959

Explanation

The Constitution of Nepal 2015 (2072 BS), promulgated on 20 September 2015, formally established Nepal as a Federal Democratic Republic. While the Republic was declared in 2008 under the Interim Constitution, the 2015 constitution institutionalized the federal structure with 3 tiers of government.

LawMedium
3. The "Grundnorm" or Basic Norm concept is associated with which jurist?
AH.L.A. Hart
BHans Kelsen
CSalmond
DAustin

Explanation

Hans Kelsen's Pure Theory of Law (Reine Rechtslehre) introduced the concept of Grundnorm (basic norm) — the highest normative authority from which all other legal norms derive their validity. It forms the apex of Kelsen's hierarchy of norms.

MCQ Q1. 2024
Law is social engineering. The proponent of this idea is:
A) Austin   B) Roscoe Pound   C) Kelsen   D) Hart
Answer: B) Roscoe Pound

Roscoe Pound's Sociological Jurisprudence views law as an instrument of social engineering to balance competing interests in society with maximum efficiency and minimum waste.

Topic: Jurisprudence | Difficulty: Easy | Source: BALLB Entrance 2024
Subjective Q2. 2024
What is the Rule of Law? Explain its significance in the context of Nepal. (10 marks)
Model Answer Points:

Definition: Rule of Law means supremacy of law over arbitrary power. A.V. Dicey identified three pillars: (1) Supremacy of law, (2) Equality before law, (3) Predominance of legal spirit.

Nepal Context: Article 1 of the Constitution of Nepal 2015 declares it as the fundamental law. Article 18 guarantees equality. The Supreme Court's judicial review power ensures rule of law.

Significance: Prevents arbitrariness, protects rights, ensures accountability of state.

Topic: Sources of Law / Constitution | Marks: 10 | Source: BALLB Entrance 2024
Creative Writing Q3. 2024
Write an essay on "Human Rights and their Importance in a Democratic Society" in approximately 300 words. (20 marks)
Model Essay Outline:

Intro: Human rights are fundamental entitlements every person possesses by virtue of being human, irrespective of nationality, religion, or status. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) established these globally.

Body: In a democratic society, human rights are the cornerstone of governance. They protect individuals from state abuse, ensure equal participation in politics, and guarantee dignity. Nepal's Constitution 2015 enshrines 35 fundamental rights, among the most comprehensive in South Asia.

Conclusion: Without human rights, democracy becomes hollow. A true democracy must not only proclaim rights but actively protect and enforce them through independent institutions.

🏛 Role of Law in Society

Law and social order, justice, development, rule of law, democracy, and social change through law.

Law and Social Order

Law maintains social order by providing a framework of rules that regulate human behavior and resolve conflicts. Without law, social cohesion would be impossible.

Law and Justice

The primary aim of law is to achieve justice. Aristotle distinguished between distributive justice (fair distribution of goods) and corrective justice (remedying wrongs).

Rule of Law

A.V. Dicey's three pillars: (1) Supremacy of law — no person is above law, (2) Equality before law, (3) Predominance of legal spirit — rights secured through ordinary courts.

Key Concepts

  • Law as instrument of social change
  • Democracy requires rule of law
  • Development depends on legal certainty
  • Nepal: Constitutional supremacy as Rule of Law
Source: TU Faculty of Law Syllabus | CollegeNP BALLB Guide | Last Updated: 2025

📜 Sources of Law

1. Constitution

The supreme source of law. All other laws must conform to the Constitution. In Nepal, the Constitution of Nepal 2015 is the grundnorm.

2. Legislation

Acts passed by Parliament. Primary legislation (Acts) and subordinate/delegated legislation (Regulations, Rules, Bylaws).

3. Judicial Precedents

Decisions of higher courts that bind lower courts (doctrine of stare decisis). Nepal's Supreme Court decisions are binding precedents under Article 128(2).

4. Custom

Long-established practices recognized as law. Must be immemorial, reasonable, certain, consistent, and compulsory.

5. International Treaties

Nepal is party to major human rights treaties. Ratified treaties are part of the law of the land. Article 278 of the Constitution governs treaties.

Source: Constitution of Nepal 2015 | TU Faculty of Law | Last Updated: 2025

🤝 Rights and Duties

Types of Rights

  • Natural Rights: Rights inherent to all humans by virtue of being human (Locke: life, liberty, property)
  • Human Rights: International standard rights under UDHR 1948
  • Legal Rights: Rights recognized and enforceable by law
  • Civil Rights: Right to equal treatment under law
  • Political Rights: Right to vote, stand for election, participate in governance

Types of Duties

  • Moral Duties: Based on conscience and ethics, not legally enforceable
  • Legal Duties: Obligations imposed by law, enforceable by state
  • Constitutional Duties: Article 48 of Nepal's Constitution: duties of citizens
Source: TU Faculty of Law Syllabus | Last Updated: 2025

👤 Person and Legal Personality

Natural Person

A human being recognized by law as having legal rights and duties. Legal capacity begins at birth (with some exceptions for unborn). Ends at death.

Legal (Juristic) Person

Non-human entities recognized by law as having legal personality: corporations, government bodies, registered institutions, universities. They can sue and be sued.

Legal Capacity

The ability to have rights (legal capacity to have rights) and the ability to exercise rights independently (legal capacity to act). Minors and mentally incapacitated persons have limited capacity to act.

Legal Liability

Civil liability arises from breach of duty causing harm. Criminal liability requires actus reus (guilty act) and mens rea (guilty mind).
